Capsaicin 0.075% Cream

Alpha-Lipoic Acid (600mg/day)

$24–$40 / 60 caps

An oral antioxidant with the strongest research base for diabetic neuropathy of anything in this list. Slow-acting (6–8 weeks) but effective as a stack with topicals. Talk to your doctor if you're on diabetes medication.

Magnesium Oil Spray

$12–$18 / 8oz

The cheap version of what Mira does. Works — but stings on broken skin and lacks the arnica/menthol synergy. Good as a maintenance option once Mira has done the heavy lifting.

Reader questions

Why did Mira beat capsaicin if capsaicin has more research?

Capsaicin's research is solid — but real-world adherence is brutal. 4 in 10 testers stopped using it within 7 days because of the initial burning. Mira's tolerability score (9.8/10) means people actually keep using it. The best treatment is the one you'll stick with.

Is Mira FDA-approved?

Mira is registered as an OTC topical lotion. It's not a drug; it doesn't claim to cure neuropathy. The active ingredients (magnesium chloride, arnica, menthol) are all FDA-recognized for topical use.

Can I use Mira with prescription meds?

It's topical and not systemically absorbed at meaningful levels, so interactions are unlikely. As always — ask your doctor before changing or stopping any prescription.
