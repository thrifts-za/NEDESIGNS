Bundle Ladder with Inline Save Flag

A 1/2/3-pack tier selector where the savings flag lives inside the row, not floating above it.
PDP+8–14% AOVShopify · subscriptions

The Idea

Most stores show 3 quantity options as separate buttons. The champ pattern shows them as a vertical ladder where each row contains: radio · qty + supply duration · save flag · price + strikethrough. The middle tier is pre-selected and visually hottest. The 3-pack row earns its position by showing the most aggressive savings.

Why it works

  • Anchoring: 1-pack price makes 2-pack feel like a steal
  • Decoy: 3-pack reframes 2-pack as the safe middle
  • Inline savings flag — no eye flicker hunting for the discount
  • Pre-selected middle tier = default for the indecisive

</div>

USE WHEN
  • 3+ qty options
  • Replenishable goods
  • Subscribe & save
SKIP WHEN
  • One-time gifts
  • Personalized SKUs
  • Premium/luxury — looks aggressive

Progressive Gift Visualization

Show the free gift inside the tier row — with thumb, name, $0.00 price, and strikethrough retail value.
PDPTrades up to higher tier

The Idea

Saying "free gift with 3-pack" in copy is forgettable. Showing the gift's product photo, name, retail value crossed out, and "$0.00" rendered in green next to it makes the gift visceral. The customer feels the gift in their cart before clicking Add.

Why it works

  • Loss aversion — they can see what they'd miss
  • Tangibility — a thumb is concrete, copy is abstract
  • Strikethrough $19 next to $0.00 stacks the value perception
+18%
Trade-up rate

2-pack → 3-pack tier when gift is visualized vs. described

The Recipe

<div class="gift-panel">

    <img src="travel-roll.png">
  </div>
  <div class="gp-body">
    <strong>Travel Roll-On</strong>
    <span>0.5oz · ships with order</span>
  </div>
  <div class="gp-price">
    <span class="free">$0.00</span>
    <span class="was">$19.00</span>
  </div>
</div>
USE WHEN
  • You actually have a giftable SKU
  • Gift is desirable on its own
  • Trade-up is the goal
SKIP WHEN
  • You'd have to invent a SKU
  • Gift is a sample-size of the same product

Auto-Marquee Reviews

A horizontally scrolling review band with side-fade masks, paused on hover.
PDP · social proof+ time-on-page

The Idea

Static review grids stop the eye. A continuous marquee with a duplicated set creates the feel of more reviews than fit on screen. Side fades hide the seam. Hover pauses for readability.

The Recipe

.marquee{overflow:hidden;
  -webkit-mask-image:linear-gradient(90deg,
    transparent,#000 8%,#000 92%,transparent)}
.marquee-track{display:flex;gap:16px;
  width:max-content;
  animation:scroll-left 60s linear infinite}
.marquee:hover .marquee-track{animation-play-state:paused}
@keyframes scroll-left{
  from{transform:translateX(0)}
  to{transform:translateX(-50%)}}

The trick

Render every card twice. The translateX(-50%) at end of animation lands on the start of the duplicate set — seamless. Mask the edges so cards fade in/out instead of pop.

4

Slide-Out Cart Drawer

A right-side panel with backdrop, free-shipping progress bar, qty stepper, and 2 upsell rows.
PDP → Cart+ AOVJS required

The Idea

Page-redirect carts kill momentum. The drawer keeps PDP context visible behind a translucent backdrop while the customer reviews. The free-shipping progress bar gamifies AOV. Two upsells live in the drawer — never on the PDP itself.

Anatomy

  • Right-aligned 420px panel, slides in 300ms
  • Backdrop dims the page; ESC and click-outside close it
  • Progress bar fills toward $50 free-ship threshold
  • Qty stepper actually mutates the active tier
  • Upsells: +Add buttons that flip in-place to ✓ Added
  • Sticky bottom: Subtotal · Savings · Secure Checkout pill

State sync (the part most stores miss)

function syncDrawer(){
  const tier = document.querySelector('.tier.active');
  const price = parseFloat(tier.dataset.price);
  let total = price;
  if(cart.upsell1) total += 19;
  if(cart.upsell2) total += 24;

  // Free shipping progress bar
  const target = 50;
  if(total >= target){
    shipText.textContent = '✓ Free shipping unlocked';
    shipFill.style.width = '100%';
  } else {
    const away = (target - total).toFixed(2);
    shipText.textContent = `$${away} away from FREE shipping`;
    shipFill.style.width = `${total/target*100}%`;
  }
}

Inline SVG Trust Strip

6 inline SVG icons in a row — no emojis, no external icon fonts.
Buy box · footerAvoid emojis

The Idea

Emojis render differently on every OS — 🌿 looks crisp on iOS, blocky on Windows. Outline SVGs render identically everywhere, can inherit currentColor, and scale to any size without fuzz.

The 6 that earn their slot

  • Shield + check — money-back guarantee
  • Truck — free shipping
  • Lab flask — clinically formulated
  • Stethoscope — doctor-formulated
  • Leaf — clean ingredients
  • Lock — secure checkout

The Recipe

<!-- Reusable currentColor SVG -->
<svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none"
   stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2">
  <path d="M12 2L4 6v6c0 5 3.5 9 8 10
            4.5-1 8-5 8-10V6l-8-4z"/>
  <path d="M9 12l2 2 4-4"/>
</svg>

Use stroke="currentColor" — the icon picks up your text color. One file, themable.

Real Payment Wordmark Row

Inline SVG wordmarks for Visa, MC, AMEX, Discover, Apple Pay, GPay, Shop Pay, Klarna.
Checkout · footer

The Idea

Most stores use logo PNGs that turn to mud on retina or get blocked by ad-blockers. Inline SVG wordmarks (or simplified shape representations) load instantly, scale crisply, and don't request external assets.

The trick for trademarks

You don't need pixel-perfect logos. A blue rectangle with "AMEX" set in 900-weight Arial reads as Amex at 24px tall. The Mastercard double-circle (red + amber overlapping) is universally recognized at 30px wide.

The Recipe

<!-- Mastercard with two circles -->
<svg viewBox="0 0 36 22" width="36" height="22">
  <circle cx="14" cy="11" r="8" fill="#EB001B"/>
  <circle cx="22" cy="11" r="8" fill="#F79E1B"
          fill-opacity=".85"/>
</svg>

Single-Page Checkout with Order Bump

Express checkout · contact · shipping · ship method + bump · payment — all on one screen.
Checkout Champ+ AOV +12–22%

The Idea

Multi-step checkouts leak conversion at every page transition. Single-page accordion-style checkouts let users see the full path, jump back to fix a typo, and trigger a one-click bump in step 4 without leaving the flow.

Where the bump goes

Tuck it inside the Shipping Method step — never in step 1 (too early) or right at payment (too late, kills conversion). Step 4 is the sweet spot: customer is committed, not yet anxious about card entry.

Bump anatomy

  • Yellow background — pattern interrupt
  • Dashed border — visual marker for "extra"
  • "One-Time Offer" flag at top-left
  • Image · checkbox-prefilled · price + strikethrough
  • Live cart total recalcs on toggle

The bump that converts

<div class="bump">
  <span class="bump-flag">One-Time Offer</span>
  <label class="bump-l">
    <input type="checkbox" checked>
    <img src="3pack.png">
    <div>
      <strong>Upgrade to 3-Pack — Save $50 more</strong>
      <p>Add 1 jar at $30 (reg. $79). Most customers
         run out by week 4.</p>
    </div>
  </label>
  <div class="bump-p">+$30<small>+$79</small></div>
</div>

Post-Purchase 1-Click Upsell

After "Order confirmed" — a separate page offering a stockpile bundle at the lowest price ever.
Checkout Champ+ LTV +$40 avg

The Idea

The order is already paid. Card is on file. Risk is gone. This is the highest-trust moment in the entire funnel — and most stores leave it on the table by going straight to a thank-you page.

The 5 elements that close it

  • Order confirmation strip — "#MIR-49281 confirmed"
  • Countdown — "Expires in 4:59"
  • The headline — "You qualify for 60% off"
  • Value stack — itemize what they get vs. retail
  • "No card re-entry" reassurance under the YES
  • Weak-link decline — "No thanks, I'll pass on saving $147"

The decline copy is the science

"No thanks" — high decline rate. "No thanks, I'll pass on saving $147 (and pay full price later)" — significantly lower decline rate. The brain processes loss before it processes thrift.

The page structure

[ Order confirmed strip · green ]
[ Countdown urgency · red    ]
[ Eyebrow: ⚡ Wait — Don't Close ]
[ H1: 60% off 3-Pack         ]
[ Sub: One click. No re-entry. ]

Live Social-Proof Ticker

A scrolling band of "Sarah J. in Austin just ordered" — between header and main content.
Checkout · PDPHonesty matters

The Idea

A continuous left-scrolling band of recent activity. First-name + first-letter-last + city + product. Pulse dot in green to signal "live."

The honesty rule

If you don't have real recent orders, don't fabricate them. Use real review excerpts in the same ticker format. Inventing fake "Sarah just ordered" pings is a regulatory and ethical landmine.

The Recipe

<div class="ticker">
  <div class="ticker-track">
    <span><i class="dot"></i>
      <b>Sarah J.</b> in Austin, TX
      just ordered the 2-pack
    </span>
    <!-- 5 more, then duplicate the set -->
  </div>
</div>

.dot{animation:pulse 1.5s ease-in-out infinite}

Sticky Mobile Pay Bar

A bottom-fixed bar on mobile checkout with total + Pay Now button — always visible.
Mobile checkoutMobile-only lift

The Idea

On desktop the order summary is sticky in a sidebar. On mobile it lives at the bottom of the page — out of sight when filling forms. The sticky pay bar keeps the total visible and gives a one-tap shortcut to submit when the user is ready.

What it shows

  • Item summary line — "2 jars + bonus"
  • Live total — updates as bump toggles
  • "Pay Now →" button — triggers submit on the real CTA

The Recipe

.sticky-mob{display:none;position:fixed;
  bottom:0;left:0;right:0;
  background:#fff;border-top:1px solid var(--line);
  padding:10px 16px;
  box-shadow:0 -8px 22px rgba(0,0,0,.08);
  z-index:50}

@media(max-width:920px){
  .sticky-mob{display:block}
  body{padding-bottom:80px} /* clear room */
}

The detail most miss: the Pay Now button shouldn't submit anything itself — it should programmatically click the real CTA in the order summary so all validation/submission logic runs once.
